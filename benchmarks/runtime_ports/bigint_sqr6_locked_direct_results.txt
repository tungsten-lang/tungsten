BIGINT SQR@6 — CLOSED-WORLD NATIVE DISPATCH REJECTION
=====================================================

Scope
-----

This is the separately measured native-only follow-up to exact sqr@6 commit
51e19a38.  The committed source seam remains active through the exact built-in
square boundary; neither rejected experiment changes its six diagonals,
fifteen doubled cross products, carry schedule, allocation class, twelve
stores, or +11/+12 result publication.

Two closed-world dispatch shapes were tested under `PROTECT_THE_CORE!` plus
`LOCK_THE_DOORS!`:

  1. an outlined size-6 test followed by the retained size-5 -> size-4 -> C
     tail chain; and
  2. one dense caller switch over sizes 1..6, with isolated active workers for
     sizes 4..6 and the unchanged exact C boundary as its default.

Both were rejected.  The first improved sqr@6 but charged earlier default-path
leaves for another serial miss.  The second removed those secondary tests but
changed the very small caller CFG enough to regress the already-faster
sqr@1..3 arms.  Production therefore retains the exact sqr@6 migration and the
previously measured sqr@1..5 locked dispatcher without adding a direct sqr@6
arm.

Validation

  - candidate and feature-off locked-direct specs pass, including positive
    and overlay-negative six-limb identities;
  - candidate and feature-off native-lane self-tests pass;
  - both compiler and benchmark variants build with release/native/fast;
  - the outlined candidate tests size 6 before its active 48-byte frame and
    tail-branches misses into the retained chain;
  - the dense candidate emits one caller comparison tree and no secondary
    size test in the isolated size-4/5/6 workers; and
  - `git diff --check` passes after restoring the production dispatcher.

Verdict
-------

REJECT both native-only dispatch changes.  Keep exact sqr@6 commit 51e19a38
and the pre-existing locked sqr@1..5 routing.  Continue the exact arithmetic
migration at sqr@7 rather than trading wins among already-migrated shapes.
