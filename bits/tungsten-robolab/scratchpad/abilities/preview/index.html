Champion

In progress v4 body: first runner in

Sprint from blocks, keyframed on Usain Bolt

Champion About 2x Bolt's time

Keyframes measured from side-view footage of Bolt: block exit, the first steps and the max-velocity cycle. The set position is the mean of the 2018 World Indoor 60 m finalists, and steps 3 to 10 are interpolated. The runner sets on two angled pads, pushes off the rear pad on its force, then the front pad, then accelerates through steps blended from Bolt's first steps into his max-velocity cycle. Reaction time is 0.237 s.

On the 95th-percentile body it covers 30 m in 6.72 s and 100 m in 19.08 s on 11 of 12 arena starts. Bolt ran 3.78 s and 9.58 s, so this is about twice his time and 4 to 6 s behind a recreational sprinter: 3.3 steps a second of 1.7 m against his 4.5 of 2.4 to 2.7 m. The same controller on the average body falls on every start. At touchdown its trunk leans about 30° more than Bolt's upright 4°.

Touch-only sprint

Champion

Your idea of keyframing only the toe-touch. One right-touchdown keyframe (the left is its mirror), each stride one spline from touch to touch, with the joint velocities at the touch, and full-power bursts timed on each leg's own touchdown and toe-off. Without the velocities every version fell within two steps.

It took over the fast-running trial: 10 of 12 starts stay up where the old champion failed all 12. The landing thigh pulse alone is worth 0.7 m/s. It runs 4.6 m/s on the average body against 5.0 for the tuned sprinter.

Jumping and hurdles

Long jump

In progress

The run-up is the sprint champion itself. World Athletics rules are in the score: foul line on every tick of the stance, no somersault past 120°, no touching the runway after landing, and the mark is the nearest break in the sand by any body part. The take-off comes from an ideal-control analysis of the runner's own approach, distilled into contraction knots latched on the plant touchdown.

That took the 95th-percentile body from 3.68 to 4.895 m on one start (99% of its 4.93 m ceiling) and from 3.22 to 3.92 m on 16 perturbed starts. The average body reaches 1.85 m robust and 2.47 m on one start.

Ball skills

Baseball swing

In progress Ceiling study shown

The earlier hitch-free swing was slow because of its plan. The arms and wrists fired at full power only 2–8% of the swing, the bottom wrist uncocked early, and the bat peaked 42 ms before contact. The fast swing turns the pelvis at full drive into its yaw limit, keeps the spine coiled, fires short arm bursts with no co-contraction, and whips the bottom wrist through 0.8 rad in the last 60 ms. The wrist runs at 1,070 °/s, the same as college hitters (1,093).

The open-loop program scores 25.2 m/s on 24 pitches it never saw and hits all 24, so it is not tuned to the 18 test pitches. Distilled into contraction knots with the reflexes on, it keeps 82–85% of that speed. Trunk and arms turn at only 50–65% of MLB rates. Landing the step 62 ms later cut the pause before the swing from 115 ms to 50 ms.

Table tennis

Blocked

Predicts the ball through the bounce, slides the pelvis onto its path, latches the ready posture on time-to-paddle and sets the paddle face from an aim law. Phase 1 is 100%. Phase 2 is 33% over 300 serves, and the winner scored 64%.

The aim is accurate. The arm does not carry it out: a forehand asked to turn its forearm 1.20 rad reaches 0.44, and the face follows only about 40% of an aimed change. Live moment arms fix the arm's tracking (backhand error 0.80 to 0.21 rad, forehand face now follows yaw one to one), but the tracked arm plays like a new player and needs a long practice run. Where the ball meets the paddle decides most forehands: 67% solved within 3 cm of the centre, 1 in 7 on the rim.

Your strokes: the chop goes high because the aim rule opens the forehand face about 40° and the forehand path cannot get steeper than about 40° down. The best chop is the backhand at a −4° face on a 40° downward path (4 cm over the net, backspin, 3 of 11 landed). The wrist flick makes topspin for the first time but only about 5 rev/s.

Next. A long practice run of the tracked arm on both sides, or a learned drive correction below the contraction layer. 90% is far above anything reached on this task.

Balance and soccer · MyoChallenge 2025 body, 290 muscles

One-leg balance

In progress

The hold is steered by the sensed centre of pressure, with a slow centring loop and per-posture muscle moment arms. The centring loop is what carries a hold past 96 s. On a 310 s trial, 12 of 20 fresh stances stay on one leg for at least 300 s, and a version with a tuned lift holds 15 of 20 (p 0.23 on 20 starts, not promoted). The 3 s trial went from 1.96 to 3.08 s.

An earlier diagnosis was wrong: the standing foot does not spin. The controller's hip model, measured at the start posture, has the wrong sign where one-leg stance happens (the gluteal moment arm ranges from −29 to +37 N·m per unit activation). All remaining failures are at the lift: the swing foot leaves the ground still carrying 20–40% of body weight, which throws the balance point past what the sole can absorb.

Next. A 40-start version of the 310 s trial so a 15-of-20 result can be confirmed, then a better lift.

Penalty kick

Phase 1 champion Phase 2 blocked

Shift weight, step, plant beside the ball, swing with a hip-flexor burst and knee snap latched on ball contact, aiming away from the keeper. Phase 1 scores every time. Phase 2 starts up to 1 m away and has 10 s, and it scores 0 of 100 (the 2023 winner scored 32%).

Two measured walls. The phase-1 kick is a trajectory tuned over seven stages from one exact start, and from a settled stance it scores 0 of 30 even on near starts. And this body cannot walk upright: holding the chest upright needs 84–123% of what the back extensors can give (the abdominal muscle's passive pull alone is 207 N·m), so the chase-tag walker folds the spine within 0.5 s.

Next. Rebuild the kick in stages from the settled stance, then a faster approach.

Search methods · finished

Genetic, random walk, or something else?

Thirteen optimizers behind one interface, equal rollout budgets, several seeds and held-out re-checks, on a jump, push-recovery balance on the 80-muscle leg, and refining the run.

QuestionAnswer
Genetic algorithm?No. GA, differential evolution, cross-entropy, MAP-Elites, Nelder-Mead and random search all failed to improve on the jump.
Random walk?Yes for ballistic moves: the (1+1) random walk had the best median jump and the most robust run refinement, but it overfits badly when trained on fresh random perturbations.
Best for choreographed motionBayesian optimisation or the random walk over goal posture + duration: 0.71 m median jump vs 0.13 m with rate vectors.
Best for robust balanceSeparable CMA-ES, trained on 4+ perturbations (falls on unseen pushes 12/30 to 3/30).
Contraction vectorsNever search raw per-muscle values; synergies distilled from good solutions converge fastest.
Keyframes, your ideaIt won. Converting the best posture solution into contraction keyframes and refining them in muscle space took the jump from 0.91 m to 1.3 to 1.5 m.
Since thenQuintic knots with velocity and acceleration vectors, a small learned correction from proprioception (legal jump 1.27 to 1.33 m with no fall on any arena start), and a gait stride as a knot train fitted by least squares then improved by learning control.
Under the hood

Bodies and arena

  • v2 anatomy: toe joints, biarticular muscles on fixed tendons, human peak joint speeds (elbow 35, knee 25, hip 15 rad/s), human-scale joint damping. Opt-in, so older results reproduce exactly.
  • v3 adds elastic Achilles and quadriceps tendons; population bodies (average, 95th percentile, elite) and the calibrated v4 body are listed above.
  • The arena holds 81 trials on fixed held-out starts. A challenger replaces the champion only when it wins a paired sign-flip test, and every result keeps the note of the approach that produced it.

SDK and compiler

  • Chunk spring times, joint corrections at limits and a dead tuning parameter were fixed in the SDK; branch, followed_by, stagger and via keyframes were added.
  • Internal-model gravity feed-forward, a clamped reflex integral (postural tone), exact posture-to-contraction geometry for coupled joints and optional articulated fingers.
  • Knot trains (RunKnotsSkill): a stride as quintic knots at its gait events, fitted with zero rollouts to 0.1 to 0.6° and 20 to 90 times better than min-jerk keyframes at the same events.
  • Compiler fixes on branches for you to merge: methods with more than 8 parameters, and parameters named t0 or t1 colliding with LLVM temporaries (which also fixed a use-after-free).
