Natural running from human keyframes

7.3°off human running, 12 starts

Contraction keyframes at touchdown, mid-stance and toe-off from human running data, eased through and latched on the feet. The latest version adds a knot at each gait event (a contraction, a velocity and an acceleration for every muscle), fitted to the target by least squares and then improved from stride-by-stride error curves (iterative learning control).

Sprint

Includes labelled stronger bodies
8.28 m/s95th-percentile body, five gears
8.41 m/slabelled sprinter body
10.11 m/slabelled beyond-human body, 476 of 512 fresh starts stay up

Full drive on the prime movers, with the opposing muscles scaled down as their partners fire (reciprocal inhibition), latched on each foot. One parameter set cannot both accelerate a standing body and cruise, so the runner has gears: above set speeds the whole stride is rebuilt from the next gear's values at the next flight. The 95th-percentile runner now has five and reaches 8.28 m/s, up from 7.45 with two.

Two what-if bodies, labelled as such and never compared with the human-profile runners. The sprinter (strength ×1.55, muscle speed ×1.45, tendon ×1.5, activation ×0.8, fixed from sprinter morphology data before any runner ran on it) reaches 8.41 m/s (8 of 12 starts stay up). The beyond-human body (strength ×2.4, muscle speed ×2.0, the same tendon and activation: an invented stretch with no measurement behind it) reaches 10.11 m/s on the starts that stay up, and 476 of 512 fresh starts do. Its first version peaked at 10.15 m/s but fell on about 30% of fresh starts; the current one keeps the speed and falls on 7.0%.

The body model was calibrated against published data along the way (the calibrated body: quadriceps series stiffness and hip speeds), which is why the same runner design scores higher on it than on the older body.

Jumping and hurdles

Long jump

Tuned to one start
5.209 mcalibrated body, tuned to one start
4.63 molder body, mean of 16 starts
4.50 mcalibrated body, mean of 32 fresh starts

The run-up is the sprint runner itself. World Athletics rules are in the score: a foul line on every tick of the stance, no somersault past 120° until landing is over, no touching the runway after landing, and the mark is the nearest break in the sand by any body part. The take-off comes from an ideal-control analysis of the runner's own approach, distilled into contraction knots latched on the plant touchdown; the landing is a table of muscle excitations locked to the predicted landing time. Both are near open loop.

The best mark is 5.209 m on one start, with programs tuned to that start: a best case, not a typical jump. Over starts no tuning ever used, the calibrated-body programs average 4.50 m over 32 (31 legal) and 3.72 m over 96 (77 legal); in a separate check of 480 fresh starts the mean was 4.00 m with 86.5% legal. On the older 95th-percentile body the one-start best is 5.122 m, the mean over 16 perturbed starts 4.63 m and over 96 fresh starts 4.32 m (89 legal).

For scale: untrained men jump 4.95 m on average and high-school state qualifiers 6.07–6.65 m. The best mark is 105% of the untrained-man mean; the fresh-start means are 75 to 92% of it.

Athletics counts the best of six attempts, and the programs above were trained for the mean mark. Trained for the best of six instead (a faster runner, a take-off that goes for the upper tail, the whole plan 0.10 m closer to the board), the same body passes the record of 8.95 m in 64% of six-attempt competitions (the 1 Oct program: 8.5%), with a median best mark of 9.08 m, from 1920 starts nobody had tuned on. The fast swing turns the pelvis at full drive into its yaw limit, keeps the spine coiled, fires short arm bursts with no co-contraction, and whips the bottom wrist through 0.8 rad in the last 60 ms. The earlier hitch-free swing was slow because of its plan: the arms and wrists fired at full power only 2 to 8% of the swing.

The current swing hits 26.5 m/s at contact on 24 held-out pitches (all hit, none stalling) and 26.83 on 48 more untouched pitches (48 of 48 hit). It is contraction knots with the reflexes on, tuned by sep-CMA on fresh training pitches; an aim correction alone turned 36 of 48 hits into 48 of 48. MLB hitters average about 32 m/s.

The same swing on a labelled 95th-percentile body (strength ×1.32, muscle speed ×1.2) reaches 30.8 m/s here and up to 32.4 m/s on 48 untouched pitches with a tuned variant, at the price of ball quality: about a fifth of its hits leave above 60°.

Through the legs. A stance planner picks a staggered lunge with the trunk twisted toward the lead leg, and catch spots that both hands can reach from behind the ball. The launch, the left hand's return and the right hand's return are open-loop tables of the 16 arm muscles, gain-scheduled on the ball's predicted crossing and the stroking arm's posture, with a low-gain tracking servo around the nominal motion of each stroke; the reflex controller drives everything else. The mean longest streak is 3.16 passes in 15 s over 25 starts (it was 1.28), with at most 5; on 32 fresh starts it is 2.75 (was 1.22), and on 128 more that nothing was tuned on 2.50 with the servo alone (2.09 before it), a streak of 3 or more on about half of the starts (the reflex controller never gets past 2). The lead leg is then lifted and turned out after the right hand's release, so the rising ball clears the thigh and finds the gate: about +0.15 more on fresh starts (2.69 against 2.59 on 128 starts nobody had used; a streak of 3 or more on 57 % of them). The two strokes of the second cycle, which were reflexes that end every long streak, are muscle tables as well: the left hand's clips the lead leg far less (87 % clean strokes against 54 %), worth about +0.3 more on starts nobody had tuned on (2.59 to 2.96 on 256 of them), a streak of 4 or more on 44 % of them (20 % before).

Crossovers. A small learned correction from the recent ball motion (Codex) took the count from 3.72 to 7.52 per 15 s run, and its round-9 version, with sustained catching, to 9.08. The trunk still pumps and the hands are rigid paddles.

Table tennis

100%phase 1

Predicts the ball through the bounce, slides the pelvis onto its path, latches the ready posture on time-to-paddle and sets the paddle face from an aim law. Phase 1 is 100%. Phase 2 is 40% over 300 serves (26.7% then 33% on 27 Sep; 44% on the earlier 100-serve set); the 2025 challenge winner scored 64%.

The latest gain came from one defect. On the single tick when the table-contact flag is set the ball's velocity is mid-reversal, and the skill predicted where the ball crosses the paddle plane from it, so for that tick the plan was 0.3 to 1.6 m off on every serve; fixing it alone lifts the hit rate from 0.80 to 0.87. A re-tuned backhand took its share on fresh serves from 21.8% to 35.9%.

Strokes measured with real ball spin: the best chop is the backhand at a 60° face and a 40° downward path (4 cm over the net, 11 rev/s of backspin, 3 of 11 landed); the wrist flick makes topspin under 5 rev/s.

One-leg balance

40 of 40hold five minutes, official trial
37 of 37more fresh starts hold five minutes

The hold is steered by the sensed centre of pressure, with a slow centring loop and per-posture muscle moment arms; the lift into single support was tuned on an 8 s proxy. On the 40-start trial (310 s episodes) 40 of 40 fresh stances stay on one leg for at least 300 s in the official, unmodified environment; the previous hold managed 34 of 40 and the one before it 24. On 37 more fresh starts it holds 37 of 37.

Most of the earlier misses were a MuJoCo bug rather than balance. When a muscle line grazes its wrap cylinder, the tendon-wrap code takes the arccosine of a value that rounds just above 1 and the muscle length becomes NaN (it reproduces on MuJoCo 3.2.7 to 3.14.0), and the swing leg's rectus femoris hovered at exactly that grazing point. The new hold parks the swing knee at an angle where none of that leg's 68 wrap segments comes near its tangent, so the official environment never meets the bug: no NaN in about 27,000 start-seconds.

Reading the numbers
  • Starts. A result is a mean over a fixed set of starting states (perturbed poses and ball or pitch positions). Fresh starts are ones that never took part in tuning. A single start is one exact state, and a program tuned to it is labelled as such.
  • Bodies. The 95th-percentile body scales published muscle data (strength ×1.32, muscle speed ×1.2, tendon stiffness ×1.5). The calibrated body adds two literature-backed changes (quadriceps series stiffness, hip speeds). The athlete body, used for the swing, has strength ×1.5. A labelled body is stronger than any of these and is never compared with them.
  • Codex. A second team runs its own challenger campaign; its policies appear where they hold the best result, and are marked.
  • Videos. Copy link copies the address of a public copy of the video on thecompanygardener.yaks.app/robolab, so it opens for anyone even though this page is private. They are renders of our simulations; no third-party footage is hosted.
