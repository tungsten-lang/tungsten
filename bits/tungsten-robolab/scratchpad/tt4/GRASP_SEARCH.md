# The grasp search: does ANY finger posture keep the paddle until contact under a fast ideal servo? (2 Oct 2026)

Erik's decision (2 Oct): run the grasp search that gates the table-tennis plane-hold stroke (`PLANE_NOTE.md`, "Verdict"). Everything below was fixed BEFORE any candidate ran.

## Question and stress test

Can the hand hold the paddle through a fast pad-space servo AND let the pad reach its plan? The stress test is T3's ideal pad-space arm servo (`playground/tasks/myo_tt_oracle.py`, mode `o5`: computed-torque PD of the 7 arm joints toward the damped-least-squares joint change that takes the pad centre and normal to the plan's) in its DECELERATING form (`o5_togo`: the fraction 2 dt / T_go of the remaining error is closed per tick, so the pad arrives at rest at the planned contact; constant deceleration, about 1.5-2 g of pad acceleration for 0.3-0.4 m in 0.2 s), plan `scratchpad/tt3/results/plan_bn0.1.json`, config `{"o5_togo": 1, "o5_tact": 0.30, "o5_hold": 0.03, "o5_wrot": 0.30}`, the champion (`bin/challengers/myo_tt_r10-n`, `params/myo_tt_p2_r10_c3.txt`) with only the grip parameters changed (`tt_eval --set`). T3 found that with the champion's grip the paddle leaves the hand before contact on 16-17 of 24 serves in this test. A LABELLED DIAGNOSTIC ORACLE: never compared with the official trials.

## Searched parameters (the grip chunk's own, with the skill's own search boxes)

`g_fpl` [-0.2, 0.6], `g_op` [-0.2, 0.6], `g_text` [-0.5, 0.3], `g_idx` [-0.2, 0.6], `g_idx_int` [-0.2, 0.6], `g_mid` [-0.2, 0.6], `g_ring` [-0.2, 0.6], `g_fext` [-0.5, 0.3], `grip_drive` [0, 2], `grip_ramp` [0.01, 0.3], `bh_grip` [-0.2, 0.4], `bh_grip_drive` [0, 0.8]. Start: the champion's values.

## Per-serve measures (from the per-tick records)

- LOST BEFORE CONTACT: the paddle site more than 0.5 m from the palm site (`palm_err`, T3's criterion) at any tick up to the contact tick. (The count over the whole episode is NOT used: the champion's losses are after the impact, see `PLANE_NOTE.md`.)
- NEVER TOUCHED: the ball never touches the paddle (an arm that throws the paddle away early must not look good).
- PAD ERROR AT CONTACT: position (cm) and orientation (deg) of the pad against the plan at the contact tick (the servo's own `pad_err`).

## Objective (minimised) on the 30 TRAINING serves, seeds 98000-98029

J = 20 x (lost before contact / serves) + 2 x (never touched / serves) + median pad position error (cm) / 2 + median pad orientation error (deg) / 3 (medians over the serves that reach contact). Each term is about 1 at the pass thresholds (5 % lost, 2 cm, 3 deg).

Method: separable CMA-ES (written here; no `cma` package), 12 candidates per generation, sigma 0.15 of the box range at the start, deterministic common serves; stop at 30 generations or 90 minutes of wall time. Candidates are run two at a time with 3 workers each, niced.

## Pass rule (the final candidate against the champion's grip, each run ONCE at the end)

On 70 VALIDATION serves (98030-98099) AND on 100 serves of T3's set B (830000-830099): (1) at most 5 % of the serves that reach contact lose the paddle before contact, (2) the median pad error at contact is at most 2 cm and at most 3 deg, (3) at least 80 % of the serves reach contact. Read as: if met, a plane-hold stroke is physically possible in this hand model and Stage 2 of `PLANE_NOTE.md` (settled arm, held plane, a short translation for the velocity vector, on live tracking, then re-practice) is worth starting (Erik's call); if not met, this is a NEGATIVE RESULT: this hand model cannot hold and steer the paddle through a servo this fast, and the table-tennis line ends.

## Stop rule

If after 30 generations or 90 minutes the best training J is not below 50 % of the champion grip's J (same serves), stop and report. Whatever happens, the final candidate's validation and set-B numbers are reported as run, together with the champion grip's, and the context numbers of the plain champion stroke with that grip (hit, solved, lost) because a grip that breaks the stroke is part of the answer.

## Baseline and AMENDMENT (2 Oct, after the baselines and before any search candidate ran)

Baselines, the champion's OWN grip under the decelerating ideal servo (`gs_champion_check.py`, `grasp_search.py baseline`): training serves (30): 26 reach contact, 0 lose the paddle before contact, pad error at contact median 0.89 cm and 2.34 deg, 14 solved; validation (70): 58 reach contact, 0 lost, 1.08 cm and 3.66 deg, 24 solved; T3's set B (100): 79 reach contact, 0 lost, 0.93 cm and 3.43 deg, 30 solved. So T3's "the paddle leaves the hand on 16-17 of 24 serves" in this servo was the whole-episode artifact (losses after the impact), and the premise of the search, that retention gates a fast servo, is false: 0 of 163 contacts lost the paddle before contact. By the letter of the pass rule the champion grip misses only narrowly (orientation 3.4-3.7 deg against 3, set-B contact fraction 79 % against 80 %). Pad speed at contact is 0.46-0.63 m/s median (the 90 % spec needs 0.2) and, even for the 49 contacts placed within 3 deg and 2 cm, only 49 % are solved; contact time is within about 5 ms of the plan's, so timing is not the cause. Six hand-chosen variants (`gs_grip_variants.py`, validation serves): the champion grip is the best of them on the return (24 solved of 58 contacts, 41 %); drive 0.8 37 %, drive 1.5 22 % (orientation error 7.5 deg), `gfull` 15 %, `gmax` 8 %, an all-zero loose grip reaches contact on only 21 of 70 serves.

Because the retention term of J is therefore inert at the champion's grip, the objective gets one more term BEFORE the search: J += 4 x (1 - solved / serves), so that the search looks for a grip that improves the RETURN under the ideal servo (what a grasp could still change: impact response, pad pose); the pass rule is evaluated as written and one criterion is ADDED: the final candidate must also beat the champion grip's solved count by at least 10 points on validation and on set B. The stop rule is unchanged.

## OUTCOME (3 Oct 2026; search 2 Oct 22:56-23:45, final 23:45-00:06; as run, once)

**Search.** 30 generations x 12 candidates (358 evaluated; a few with a NaN pad error, ranked last), separable CMA-ES, 30 training serves (98000-98029). Best training J 2.6705 (generation 25, candidate 1) against the champion grip's 3.6249: 26 % lower, so the stop rule (best J below 50 % of the champion's) is NOT met: "stop and report". On the training serves the best grip reaches contact on 29 of 30 (champion 26), solves 20 (champion 14), pad error 0.81 cm and 2.6 deg (champion 0.89 cm, 2.34 deg). Best of 358 on 30 serves is an optimistic number: the held-out sets below decide. Best grip (champion's value in brackets): `g_fpl` 0.497 (0.414), `g_op` 0.108 (-0.071), `g_text` -0.306 (-0.244), `g_idx` 0.396 (0.373), `g_idx_int` 0.557 (0.467), `g_mid` 0.507 (0.398), `g_ring` 0.569 (0.101), `g_fext` 0.205 (0.165), `grip_drive` 0.217 (0.120), `grip_ramp` 0.100 (0.096), `bh_grip` -0.007 (0.164), `bh_grip_drive` 0.662 (0.800); the largest change is a firm ring finger.

**Verdict by the letter of the rule (with the amendment fixed before the search ran).** The champion grip fails (orientation 3.66 and 3.43 deg against 3; set-B contact 79 % against 80 %). The best candidate passes all four criteria on both sets: nothing lost, median pad error 1.0 cm / 2.7 deg and 0.9 cm / 2.3 deg, contact 83 % and 82 %, and 11 more serves solved on validation (+16 points) and 10 more on set B (+10 points, exactly the amendment's threshold). So a grasp exists that holds the paddle and lets the ideal servo steer it to 1 cm and 2.3-2.7 deg: by the pre-registered reading a plane-hold stroke is physically possible in this hand model.

**Context numbers (the part that matters for a real stroke).** The PLAIN champion stroke (no oracle, the real muscle excitations) on the 70 validation serves: with the champion grip 62 reach contact, **30 solved**; with the best grip 59 reach contact, **11 solved**. The grip found under the ideal servo BREAKS the practised stroke, whose excitations were tuned to the old grip. Also: the practised stroke with its own grip (30 of 70, 43 %) already beats the ideal arm servo with the champion grip (24 of 70, 34 %) and sits within 5 serves of it with the best grip (35 of 70): the still-pad plan of the oracle is not better than the practised stroke's own impact, so the oracle's gain from the grip is not a gain over what the champion already returns. What the result does show: the hand model has a grip that returns about 15 points more than the champion's at the same ideal pad errors, and whether a re-practised muscle stroke can use it is a new experiment (interpolation scan `gs_plain_scan.py`, below).

**Follow-ups after the verdict (3 Oct; not part of the pre-registered rule, reported because they decide what the verdict means).**
- Interpolating the twelve grip parameters from the champion's grip (alpha 0) to the best candidate (alpha 1), PLAIN champion stroke, 70 validation serves (`gs_plain_scan.py`): solved 30 / 25 / 22 / 19 / 11 of 70 at alpha 0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1 (contacts 62 / 59 / 61 / 60 / 59). The practised stroke loses steadily with any change of the grip: no part of the new grip carries over without a re-practice.
- The new grip with an IDEAL BODY (T3's settled pelvis `o1` + the decelerating ideal arm `o5`, `gs_ideal_best.py`): validation 38 of 70 solved (60 contacts, 0.7 cm, 2.2 deg, pad speed 0.49 m/s) and set B 46 of 100 (82 contacts, 0.8 cm, 2.0 deg, 0.39 m/s), against 34 of 70 and 50 of 100 for the champion grip with the same ideal body (`gs_pelvis_check.py`): +4 and -4, inside the +-3 serves of run-to-run noise. So the +11 / +10 of the verdict exist only against the champion's own (moving) pelvis; with a settled body both grips return about half (49-54 % of validation, 46-50 % of set B), against 97 % for the kinematic wall on the same plan.
- Two more diagnostics (3 Oct) say where the ~50 % comes from: (1) a paddle WELDED rigidly to the actual palm (oracle `o6`, `gs_weld_check.py`) with the ideal arm reaches contact on 69 of 70 and 97 of 100 serves (the hand-held paddle: 58 and 79) but returns no more per contact (champion pelvis: 25 of 70 and 42 of 100; settled pelvis: 31 of 70 and 40 of 100; pad error 0.9-1.2 cm and 2.7-3.0 deg, pad speed 0.84-0.99 m/s), so the hand-held paddle's response is NOT what limits the return (a rigid attachment is no better; the hand-held paddle with the settled pelvis returns 56-63 % of its contacts); the limit is the pad's residual speed (0.4-1 m/s against the 0.2 m/s the 90 % spec needs) and orientation (2.3-3 deg) at the instant of contact, which the decelerating servo cannot remove because contact time jitters by 10-30 ms against a deceleration of about 2 g; (2) a damped-hold variant of the held plane (the pad arrives 0.05-0.15 s early and holds with a step of 0.3-0.6 of the remaining error per tick; `gs_hold_check.py`, welded paddle, settled pelvis, validation): lead 0.05 s solves 36 of 70 (pad 2.2 cm, 5.5 deg, 0.61 m/s; against 31 of 70 without the lead), lead 0.10 s 19-23 of 70, lead 0.15 s 13 of 70 (41 contacts): the damped hold does not converge onto the plan, so no oracle here shows a clean held plane. A constant-velocity pass (a designed pad velocity, so that the speed at contact does not depend on the contact time) is the untested design that would address the residual speed; it needs a new plan family and servo, and nothing found so far suggests a muscle-level stroke would approach the oracle.
- **The oracle ladder (3 Oct; solved serves, validation 70 / set B 100; every row after the first two is a LABELLED DIAGNOSTIC ORACLE, never comparable with the official trial; scripts `scratchpad/tt4/gs_*.py`, oracle modes o5/o6/o7 in `playground/tasks/myo_tt_oracle.py`):** the champion's plain muscle stroke 30 / 70 (the official trial: 40.0 %); ideal arm servo, champion pelvis, hand-held paddle 24 / 70 and 30 / 100; plus a settled pelvis 34 / 70 and 50 / 100; a rigid weld of the paddle to the palm instead of the hand-held paddle 31 / 70 and 40 / 100 (no better); a HELD PLANE (the pad arrives 0.05 s before the planned contact and holds with a damped step, paddle welded from t = 0, settled pelvis, trunk locked from 0.25 s, the champion's arm muscles still on) 40-48 / 70 (57-69 %), pad speed 0.4-0.6 m/s; the same with the arm muscles switched off while the servo runs (the ideal servo is the arm's only actuator) **47 / 70 and 63 / 100 (67 % and 63 %)**, pad speed 0.06-0.07 m/s, 0.5 cm, 2.1-2.3 deg (with gravity feed-forward 49 / 70 and 65 / 100, 0.2-0.3 m/s, 1.7-1.8 deg); the kinematic wall on the same plan 97 %. For the stillest held-plane run (validation, 68 contacts) the return depends on the pad's orientation error at contact: at most 1 deg 9 of 10 solved, 1-2 deg 18 of 23, 2-3 deg 16 of 22, 3-5 deg 1 of 6, above 5 deg 3 of 7; position error matters less (above 2 cm 1 of 6). The 1-4 deg floor is the wrist's JOINT LIMITS: at the floors the wrist flexion sits at -0.88 rad (range +-0.79) or the deviation at 0.53 (range -0.17 to 0.44) and the servo, unaware of limits, pushes the joint through its soft limit; a limit-respecting servo (bounded least squares, `o5_limits`) reaches only 37-51 of 70 serves, because the champion's stance leaves some plans outside the arm's reach. So even an oracle with every ideal (rigid paddle, settled body, locked trunk, an arm servo that is the only actuator, a plan with foreknowledge) returns about 65 %, the 2025 winner's level and well above the champion's 40 %, but not 90 %: 90 % needs the orientation within about 1 deg on nearly every serve, which needs a stance and redundancy planner that keeps the wrist mid-range, and the oracle's arm servo is not something muscles can do. Recommendation: the plane-hold idea is right in what it says (orientation precision at contact is what decides the return), but the table-tennis line does not reach 90 % by it; the next lever, if Erik wants one, is the stance / wrist-range planner, then a muscle-level realization.
- Integrity of the oracle module after the o6 / o7 / hold / limits edits (all opt-in): `scratchpad/tt3/identity.py` with the switch off: 6 of 6 serves identical to the official task (standard and fast paths); mode o5 alone reproduces its earlier validation numbers exactly (58 contacts, 26 solved, 1.07 cm, 3.51 deg, 0.59 m/s).
