## Stedman

A vertex represents a six in the ringing composition. Its type and call determine its successor and the type required there. The successful 420-six encoding has six types and two calls per vertex. It also has shortened transition guards, partially specified destination types and 748 units fixing call or type bits. The header alone admits two decompositions of the variables. Wassat uses the clauses to choose a layout. [Ringing and SAT encodings](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/pp38/open)

After applying the pins and checking local type support, 1,208 transitions survive and 214 vertices already have a single choice. If a destination can only be reached from one source, that source must go there. If a source has committed to a destination, other sources must go elsewhere. Applying these rules with destination-type compatibility fixes a single 420-cycle in nine rounds, without branching on a call. Original-CNF completion takes 231 decisions and zero conflicts. An independent search over a cycle cover recovered the same cycle length. ## Ramsey upper bound on 18 vertices

Wassat verifies all 816 triangle clauses and all 18,564 six-set clauses using their edge variables and combinatorial ranks. The existing parity-refined recurrence gave the upper bound 19. The new check recovers a smaller upper bound from neighborhood degree constraints. This is a computation of a classical result. Graver and Yackel describe the same 5-regularity and 12-vertex neighborhood structure. [Graver and Yackel](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800%2868%2980038-9) · [Native neighborhood check](../lib/ramsey_neighborhood.w)

Let $L(k,n)$ be a lower edge bound for triangle-free graphs on $n$ vertices with independence number less than $k$. Deleting the closed neighborhood of a degree-$d$ vertex leaves $n-1-d$ vertices and lowers the independence bound by one. Its neighbors are independent, so the number of remaining edges equals the original edge count minus the sum of their degrees. Summing over roots yields

$$
ne - \sum_v d(v)^2 \ge \sum_v L(k-1,n-1-d(v)).
$$

Starting with $k=2$, Wassat enumerates degree histograms satisfying this inequality and the handshake lemma. The only surviving profile for $k=6,n=18$ is 5-regular with 45 edges. For any root the twelve vertices outside its closed neighborhood therefore span 20 edges. Their only possible profile has eight degree-three and four degree-four vertices. The bounds of ten edges on eight vertices and six on seven make the summed inequality tight. Each low vertex has one high neighbor and two low neighbors. Each high vertex has two high and two low neighbors. The highs form a four-cycle, and the lows form disjoint cycles. For every nonedge in the original graph, the two endpoints must have one or two common neighbors. [Computed bounds](../../../build/wassat-argument-stedman-20261118/ramsey-deficiency.json)

Label the five neighbors of the root. Each high vertex meets exactly one of them. The four choices are distinct because adjacent highs exclude triangles and opposite highs already have two common neighbors. Each low vertex meets two of the five. Repeating a pair would give its members three common neighbors, including the root. The two unused pairs must form a matching on the four labels occupied by highs. There are three matchings. Across the three, Wassat enumerates three possible low cycle covers and 72 triangle-free completions. Every completion violates the common-neighbor bound. [Independent enumeration](../../../build/wassat-argument-stedman-20261118/ramsey-local.json) · [Original-clause coverage profile](../../../build/wassat-argument-stedman-20261118/ramsey-native.profile)

For an independent Boolean check, each matching fixes 93 original edges, leaving all low-to-high and low-to-low edges free. CaDiCaL produced a DRAT refutation of each original CNF augmented by those 93 units. `drat-trim` verified all three. The reduction to these exhaustive cases is the mathematical argument above. With a direct DRAT request and a one-conflict limit, Wassat returns UNKNOWN. The structural lane also declines an input with one missing clique hidden by a duplicate, and a one-color mutation has a checked SAT verdict in the normal solver. [Branch proof checks](../../../build/wassat-argument-stedman-20261118/ramsey-replay.json) · [Mutations and proof-mode control](../../../build/wassat-argument-stedman-20261118/ramsey-tests.json)

## Case 387, uniqueness of an inverse

The 40-bit circuit asks whether two distinct inputs can have equal products with the same odd word modulo `2^40`. If their lowest differing bit is bit `k`, their difference is an odd multiple of `2^k`. Multiplication by another odd number leaves bit `k` set in the difference. Equal products therefore force the inputs to agree in every bit.

Wassat recognizes paired gate definitions and checks the corresponding equality one bit at a time. Equality propagates forward through paired AND gates. Working backwards through an XOR with equal outputs and a common operand recovers equality of the other operand. When necessary, the proof considers both values of that common bit. Unit propagation checks every proposed implication against earlier lemmas and the original clauses before Wassat uses it. The last step contradicts the input mismatch. [Native proof generator](../lib/paired_circuit.w)

## Cases 364–366, how many new arguments a defense needs

Every solution must accept additional arguments beyond the grounded extension. The following lower bounds hold on the unmodified competition CNFs. Selecting an argument obliges its defenders to defeat each of its attackers. Give each outstanding obligation an integer weight. If a possible defender covers at most $c$ units of weight, a total weight greater than $cr$ requires at least $r+1$ further arguments. Each defense obligation is checked by unit propagation on the original clauses under the selected arguments. Adding the weighted inequalities to the assumed cardinality bound rules out that selection. Once a root is ruled out, subsequent roots have fewer possible defenders. Case 364 also uses branching on the possible defenders of an uncovered attacker. ## Cases 336 and 337, triangle-free Ramsey graphs

Case 336 encodes 23 vertices and case 337 encodes 24. A satisfying assignment would give a triangle-free graph with no independent seven-set. Both color families must cover every required vertex subset. Wassat checks each original clause and its combination rank before accepting a proof. A second checker reconstructed both original files clause by clause. [Coverage and release measurements](../../../build/wassat-ramsey-degree-20261118/validation.json)

For case 337, deleting a vertex and its neighbors leaves a graph with no independent six-set. If a vertex has degree $d$, the removed edges number the sum of the degrees of its neighbors because the neighbors are independent. Summing over all vertices gives the inequality $ne \ge \sum_v (d_v^2 + L_{k-1}(n-1-d_v))$, where $L$ is a lower bound on the number of edges in the smaller graph. The dynamic program minimizes the right side at every even degree sum. It rules out 24 vertices. At 23 vertices it still permits 66 through 69 edges. [Native implementation](../lib/ramsey_neighborhood.w) · [Radziszowski and Kreher](https://cs.rit.edu/~spr/PUBL/paper18.pdf)

For case 336, Wassat generates all triangle-free graphs with no independent five-set through order 14 by extending each smaller graph by one vertex. Exact isomorphism tests leave twelve representatives on 12 vertices, one on 13, and none on 14. Gluing a minimum-degree root and its neighbors onto these representatives proves that any triangle-free 17-vertex graph with no independent six-set must have at least 40 edges. The 40-edge positive control has an independently checked graph. [Generator](../lib/ramsey_seeds.w) · [Boundary checks](../../../build/wassat-ramsey-degree-20261118/boundaries.json)

The earlier 18-vertex refutation forces degrees 5 or 6 in a hypothetical 23-vertex graph. If $s>0$ vertices had degree five, the triangle-free graph they induce has average degree at most $s/2$ by Mantel's theorem. Choose one meeting at most that many other degree-five vertices. Its 17 nonneighbors have at most $(138-s)/2-30+s/2=39$ edges, contradicting the 40-edge bound. All vertices must therefore have degree six.

Each nonedge has at least one common neighbor, since each of the 16 vertices outside a closed neighborhood has degree at most five there. If no pair had three common neighbors, distributing 30 length-two walks over these 16 vertices would give exactly 14 four-cycles through every vertex. The total incidence count $23\cdot14$ is indivisible by four. Choose a nonedge with maximum common-neighbor count $c\ge3$. The $9+c$ vertices outside both closed neighborhoods have no independent five-set, so their graph is one of the generated 12- or 13-vertex representatives. Exhausting the possible attachments rules out every completion. This is a finite structural refutation in fast mode. [Native gluing search](../lib/ramsey_gluing.w) · [Graver and Yackel’s four-cycle argument](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800(68)80038-9)

For any proposed root neighborhood, delete internal edges in that neighborhood and add the root. This retains every edge of the hypothetical graph and creates no triangle.
