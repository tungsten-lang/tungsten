# Dominance standing, 2026-07-27

Measured with `benchmarks/reference.py` against the installed rivals
(CaDiCaL 3.0.1, CryptoMiniSat 5) on an M5 Max, 6 performance + 12 efficiency
cores, at commit `8084b2e`. Settings, because two of them matter:

```
REPS=1  TOLERANCE=1.5x  SURVEY_BUDGET=120  COMP_BUDGET=60  (all defaults)
SATLIB_ROOT -> a shim of symlinks into /tmp/satbench-ext (see caveats)
```

Parity and survey come from a full `reference.py` run; the competition tier was
run separately through the suite's own `survey_section`/`scoreboard` on the
`COMPETITION` table, so every tier is scored by the same code.

## Headline

| tier | result |
|---|---|
| parity | 9 of 10 present rows `[ok]`, one `[SLOW]` |
| survey | geomean **0.73x** over 36 rows / 23 families — 13 W, 1 T, 14 L, 8 unsolved |
| competition | geomean **0.66x** over 24 rows / 20 families — 4 W, 3 T, 9 L, 8 unsolved |
| **survey + competition** | **geomean 0.71x** over 60 rows / 43 families, 44 scored |
| frontier | none configured (`LR5_37` / `LR5_41` unset) |

**Still behind on breadth, and that is the number that counts.** The
comparable figure is the two breadth tiers **together**: 36 + 24 = 60 rows and
23 + 20 = 43 families is exactly the "60 rows / 43 families" the previously
recorded 0.63x was computed over. So the like-for-like reading is
**0.63x → 0.71x**, and even that should be held loosely: this run is `REPS=1`,
the row lists may have shifted, and no attempt was made to reproduce the
original conditions. Quote the survey tier alone (0.73x) only when you say so.

**The deficit is dominated by not finishing, not by being slow.** 16 of 60
rows are unsolved inside budget, and unsolved rows never enter the geomean at
all (`reference.py:423`) — so 0.71x is computed over the 44 rows wassat
actually completed and *already excludes* its worst outcomes. CaDiCaL solved
every unsolved-by-wassat row except `lymphosat-only/16`, which nobody solved.

## The budget artifact — do not quote a short-budget geomean

The same binary, same rows, only the survey budget changed:

| survey budget | geomean | rows wassat failed to solve |
|---|---|---|
| 45s | **1.04x** | 11 |
| 120s (default) | **0.73x** | 8 |

Unsolved rows never enter the geomean (`reference.py:423`). A short budget
therefore *drops wassat's worst rows out of its own scoreboard* and flatters
it: going 45s -> 120s let wassat finish three more instances, all of them
slowly, and those three arrived as 0.01x, 0.14x and 0.30x. Always report the
budget alongside the geomean, and prefer the default.

## Caveats, stated rather than buried

1. **`REPS=1`.** Single measurement per row. Fine for ranking families,
   too noisy for row-level claims under 20%.
2. **SATLIB shim.** `SATLIB_ROOT` was reconstructed as symlinks into
   `/tmp/satbench-ext`, because SATLIB proper is not on this machine.
   `uuf100-01` does not exist anywhere on disk and is reported MISSING —
   that is one of the suite's two failures and is an artifact of the shim,
   not a regression.
3. **The other failure is real but pre-existing:** `bmc-ibm-12` is `[SLOW]`
   in the parity gate (0.70s against cms5's 0.19s, outside the 1.5x
   tolerance). It is *not* caused by the arm-count change landed in
   `8084b2e` — measured directly, that row is 0.738s at the new width 4
   against 0.804s under the old clause-count ladder, i.e. the change made it
   slightly faster.
4. **The published field columns are not a ratio against our clock** —
   competition hardware, 5000s budget. They rank the field only.
5. **~2.3 foreign cores were busy** (a browser). Contention penalises wassat's
   multi-armed race more than it penalises the single-threaded rivals, so
   these ratios are a lower bound on wassat.
