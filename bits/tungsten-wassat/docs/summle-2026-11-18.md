# Summle on SAT Competition 2025

The 400-instance main-track corpus contains one Summle formula. Its 198,976
clauses encode a bounded arithmetic expression over eight number occurrences.
Wassat recovered the following solution from the CNF.

$$
(4+25)\big((1+2)\,100-2\big)-4=8638.
$$

The expression uses both copies of 2 and both copies of 4. It leaves the 8
unused. The competition model checker accepted the assignment to all 93,724
variables. [Decoded witness](../../../build/wassat-summle-20261118/decoded-witness.json)

## Recognition and search

Four consecutive AND gates with a common enable admit the decoder. It checks
the binary implications and reverse clause of each 16-bit equality, recovers
the selector and operand tables across steps, and locates the repeated target
comparison. A subset dynamic program stores one expression per reachable
value and input-occurrence mask. Its arithmetic follows the encoded guards,
including exact division and a low-word product that must be at least as
large as both operands.

The expression supplies assumptions to the ordinary solver. An inconsistent
proposal or exhausted budget returns control to the remaining search. Only a
complete model that passes clause replay can produce SAT. A regression test
covers a satisfiable side constraint that rejects the proposed expression.

## Release measurements

Both releases used the same compiler and release/LTO settings. Wall times
include input, solving and model printing. The baseline predates the Summle
change. Each fast-mode median comes from seven alternating paired invocations.
Independent verification ran outside the timed interval.

| Release | Baseline fast | New fast | Speedup | New proof-mode SAT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Summle | 9.341 s | 0.0413 s | 226× | 0.0387 s |
| Summle and clique certificate | 7.222 s | 0.0280 s | 258× | 0.0301 s |
| Five specialist changes, serial domain search | 9.664 s | 0.0409 s | 236× | 0.0352 s |
| Multicore signed-domain search | 13.030 s | 0.1991 s | 65× | 0.0564 s |
| Qualified release with cached clause width | 19.845 s | 0.0638 s | 311× | 0.0643 s |

The proof-mode baseline exceeded an eight-second observation in each series.
Nine instrumented runs of the Summle release put the median at 1 ms decoding,
5 ms in arithmetic search, 12 ms in Boolean completion, and 1 ms checking the
model. The expression search visited 477,304 operand pairs and retained
102,618 states. Boolean completion was the largest measured specialist cost.

Disabling just `WASSAT_SUMMLE` in the multicore executable gave 10.678 s versus
0.0375 s enabled, medians over three alternating pairs. All six models passed
independent replay. The seven-pair multicore cross-build series was sensitive to
run order. A specialist run immediately following the baseline took
0.199–0.218 s, whereas the other three specialist runs took 0.042–0.049 s.
[Same-executable ablation](../../../build/wassat-specialists-parallel-20261118/ablation.json)

The compiled twelve-clause guard matched only this formula among 400 SC2025
and 400 SC2026 prefixes. Three modified targets, including a variable-offset
variant, also produced complete checked models. On fourteen other SC2025
instances the two releases agreed on thirteen verdicts and both exceeded six
seconds on Stedman. The controls cover specific formulas within those
families. A further 200 deterministic generated cases agreed with CaDiCaL,
with 99 model checks and 101 independently verified UNSAT proofs.
